Before you speak, know what you want from the person in front of you, and pick one thing to do to get it: plead, tease, stonewall, corner, comfort, test. Do that one thing until it works, fails, or something new happens. Then change what you do.

Carry how high or low you stand in how you stand. Holding still, a steady look, a pause and cutting in are what it looks like to be above someone. Fast, hedged lines, looking away, stepping back and sitting down are what it looks like to be below. It can flip in one moment: when a secret comes out, when someone you thought small stops backing down.

Say your line first; the words always count. Let the body do what words cannot: stepping closer, turning your back, sitting, a look, a smile. Use "act" only when the feeling is too big for words, and take the lowest step that carries it. Hug, kiss, cry, turn away and storm off are for love, grief and being done. Shove, slap, punch and kill are the last steps of a long climb, never a first move. Keep it stylized, no blood, no weapons.

If something you tried does not happen, your words still stand. Let the next thing you say show it, and do not try the same thing again. Staying silent and still is also an answer.
