## Frameworks

### Big Five / IPIP — the backbone

The canonical questionnaire uses the five-factor model as its primary lens: social energy (extraversion), cooperation (agreeableness), emotional stability, organization (conscientiousness), and openness to experience. IPIP is the public-domain item source for measures in this space: free scales, facet-level items, and translations, maintained as a scientific collaboratory rather than a proprietary instrument. BFI-2 (below) is the modern fixed-form operationalization of the same five domains with 15 facets.

### BFI-2

Soto & John's revised Big Five Inventory addresses bandwidth problems of the original BFI: 60 items, 15 facet scales (3 per domain), with facet scores that preserve the domain structure while adding fidelity — the practical instrument for the canonical interview flow, where labeled domains are too coarse for persona synthesis.

### Interpersonal Circumplex

The social layer of the hybrid model: trait-descriptive terms organized around two orthogonal axes — dominance vs. submissiveness and warmth vs. coldness — so "positioning toward others" (leading vs. deferring, conciliatory vs. confrontational) is a location on a circle, not a list of labels. This is the direct antecedent of the circumplex geometry the emotion architecture also uses (see [emotion.md](../architecture/emotion.md)).

### HEXACO

Ashton & Lee's six-factor model adds Honesty-Humility to the five-factor space: directness, humility, vanity, fairness, status-seeking, and the relationship with truth/convenience — the dimensions the canonical guide lists as needing careful observation without moral judgment. It is treated as a complement that refines traits the five-factor model captures indistinctly, never as a replacement backbone.
