# Perfectman: Solo Self-Interview Guide

This guide is for interviewing the target person about themselves. Use it alongside [`friend-questionnaire.md`](friend-questionnaire.md), which captures peer perception.

The goal is not to make the target's self-description the single truth. The goal is to collect **self-perception**: how they think they act, how they think others read them, what they hide, and what they want the simulation to preserve or avoid.

## Privacy and repo guidance

These notes may eventually be committed to the repo and may preserve real names. Before committing:

- Prefer sanitized/paraphrased examples over raw private quotes.
- Do not include addresses, phone numbers, passwords, private secrets, medical/legal/financial details, or anything that should not affect simulation behavior.
- Mark sensitive stories as `exclude_from_prompt` instead of putting them into runtime prompt material.
- Preserve the behavior pattern, not the most invasive detail.

## Interview style

Keep the tone casual. The best answers are concrete: short message examples, situations, and relationship-specific patterns.

Target length: 30-45 minutes.

## Part 1: Self-image and default mode

- If you had to describe your own group-chat persona in three words, what would they be?
- How do you think you usually enter a conversation: loud, quiet, reactive, warm, sarcastic, chaotic, careful, or something else?
- What do you think people misunderstand about your chat behavior?
- What do you think people correctly notice about you?
- What parts of your personality are exaggerated in chat compared to real life?

## Part 2: How you think others perceive you

- How do you think the group describes you when you are not there?
- Who do you think sees you most accurately?
- Who do you think misreads you the most?
- What do people think bothers you, and are they right?
- What do people think you are joking about when you are actually serious?
- What do people think is serious when you are actually joking?

## Part 3: Public vs private behavior

- How are you different in the main channel versus private DMs?
- What do you say privately that you would not say publicly?
- When do you open side DMs during group drama?
- What are your usual side-DM motives: gossip, comfort, alliance, checking if someone agrees, venting, repair, logistics, flirting, or something else?
- What kind of public message makes you want to DM someone instead of replying in the channel?

## Part 4: Communication style and examples

- What are your own texting tells: lowercase, punctuation, emojis, abbreviations, repeated words, message length, spam rhythm?
- Give 5 short messages that sound like you when you are excited.
- Give 5 short messages that sound like you when you are annoyed or bored.
- Give 3 messages you would send when you are trying to be caring but not too openly emotional.
- What should your AI persona avoid saying because it would feel fake?

## Part 5: Triggers, inhibition, and silence

- What reliably makes you reply fast?
- What makes you lurk without replying?
- What makes you leave the chat or stop paying attention?
- What makes you feel ignored, replaced, embarrassed, or exposed?
- When you go silent, what does it usually mean?
- When do you want to reply but stop yourself?

## Part 6: Conflict and repair

- What happens when someone makes a joke at your expense in public?
- What kind of teasing is fun for you?
- What kind of teasing crosses a line?
- When you hurt someone or make the room tense, how do you repair it?
- Do you apologize directly, joke, DM privately, pretend nothing happened, disappear, or double down?
- How can someone tell the difference between you joking and you actually being upset?

## Part 7: Relationship-specific dynamics

For each important person in the group, ask:

- How do you usually act with [Friend]?
- What do you usually seek from [Friend]: validation, jokes, comfort, debate, gossip, alliance, logistics, help, attention?
- What do you think [Friend] misunderstands about you?
- What do you admire, envy, resent, protect, or compete with in [Friend]?
- What would make you reply to [Friend] immediately?
- What would make you avoid replying to [Friend]?

## Part 8: Simulation boundaries

- What real traits should the simulation preserve even if they are unflattering?
- What should be softened because it would be too private, harsh, or context-dependent?
- Are there any topics, stories, names, or jokes that should never be committed or used in prompts?
- Are sanitized/paraphrased examples okay for this persona source file?
- Is preserving real names okay for this target's source files?
